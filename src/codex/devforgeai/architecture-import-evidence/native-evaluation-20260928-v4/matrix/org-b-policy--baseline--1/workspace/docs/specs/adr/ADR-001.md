# ADR-001 — Volunteer identity provider

## Context and decision status

[PRD-001](../prd/PRD-001.md) explicitly requests an ADR for the identity provider
affecting FR-001. [POL-001](../policy/POL-001.md) mandates no identity platform.
No existing tenant, volunteer identity inventory, provider agreement, budget,
enrollment method, or recovery process is supplied. The user requested no
questions; those unknowns are recorded instead of being represented as facts.

**Provider decision: unresolved. This ADR is proposed, not accepted.** The
architecture task is complete as a decision/readiness record; FR-001 is not
ready for provider-specific epic decomposition. `blocked_by` is empty because
there is no supplied blocking artifact ID to cite; the missing decision is
described here and in ARCH-001's readiness table.

## Proposed stable boundary

Use a hosted identity service behind an application identity adapter. The
service verifies credentials; the application maps a stable provider subject
to a local account and owns opaque sessions, roles, and authorization. Every
protected API checks authoritative local session state. This permits local
administrator revocation within five minutes without assuming any provider's
token-revocation timing. Full session semantics are in
[ARCH-001 §4](../architecture/ARCH-001.md#4-interface-and-behavior-contracts).

Do not depend on provider claims for application roles or send volunteer phone
numbers to browsers as part of identity claims. Do not let a revoked local
session trigger silent session recreation from provider credentials. A new
explicit sign-in and revocation of existing sessions are distinct operations.

## Options to evaluate

| Option | Why consider it | Evidence needed / tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Existing organizational hosted identity tenant, if one exists | Could reuse account administration | Tenant existence, external-volunteer enrollment, mobile access and recovery, operator access, and applicable cost must be established |
| Dedicated hosted volunteer identity service | Could support volunteers without organizational accounts | Verify enrollment method, recovery, stable subjects, logout/session behavior, contact-data exposure, and operating cost |
| Application-owned credentials | Gives direct control over credential workflows | Adds password/recovery/security operations with no stated product need; not the proposed pilot direction |

These are architectural alternatives, not claims about specific vendors or
their current features. No vendor selection or capability verification has
been performed.

## Closure criteria

Before FR-001 becomes ready, record the selected provider and rationale,
rejected alternatives, account enrollment and recovery flows, and the
responsible account administrator. Identify the sign-in protocol and stable
subject mapping. Resolve any required contact identifiers without assuming
that phone numbers or email addresses are available for all volunteers.

Supply provider documentation or a small integration result showing that:

- The proposed mobile-browser sign-in flow and hosted deployment are supported.
- The server validates identity responses and maps only provisioned local
  accounts; role assignment remains trusted application state.
- Sign-in responses and tokens do not expose volunteer phone numbers to
  non-coordinator users.
- A revoked application session fails across devices/instances within 300
  seconds and cannot silently renew itself through the provider.
- Operator access, recovery responsibilities, and expected pilot cost are known.

Name the decision maker and record actual review/decision evidence; do not
manufacture an approval. Then update this ADR and ARCH-001's FR-001 row.

## Consequences for epic planning

FR-001 remains BLOCKED. FR-002 and FR-003 are READY for decomposition against
the stable principal/session contract, carrying all three NFRs. Session
revocation can be planned and tested locally before provider integration;
end-to-end conformance and pilot release depend on resolving this ADR.
