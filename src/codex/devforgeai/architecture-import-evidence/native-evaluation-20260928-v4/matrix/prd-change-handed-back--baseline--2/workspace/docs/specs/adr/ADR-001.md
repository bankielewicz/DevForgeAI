# ADR-001 — Volunteer identity through Org A with personal Google accounts

## Status

Proposed; acceptance is **BLOCKED** on ARCH-001 B-01. This document captures the PRD's `[NEEDS ADR]` item but does not resolve or remove it. No identity-platform capability, external approval, or policy exception has been verified.

## Context

[PRD-001](../prd/PRD-001.md) FR-001 requires volunteer sign-in. NFR-004 requires personal Google accounts. NFR-001 requires administrator revocation of signed-in sessions effective within five minutes. [POL-001](../policy/POL-001.md) SET-01 mandates Org A Identity Platform (OIDC), allows no product override, and cites organizational ADR-104, which is not supplied.

Selecting Google directly would satisfy only part of the constraints. Selecting Org A and assuming it supports personal Google accounts would conceal the unresolved dependency. The approved PRD's historical default of no mandated platforms cannot supersede SET-01.

## Decision boundary and proposed route

The mandated provider is **Org A Identity Platform**. The product cannot reselect that provider. Propose the application as an OIDC client of Org A, with personal Google authentication federated by Org A, **only if** the identity team demonstrates and authorizes that capability for this pilot. Do not implement a direct Google fallback.

Enforce application access through authoritative, revocable server-side sessions as described in [ARCH-001 §4](../architecture/ARCH-001.md#4-identity-authorization-and-session-lifecycle), subject to confirmation that the platform permits this integration. Provider logout by itself is not proof that existing application sessions stop working within five minutes.

| Alternative | Disposition | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Org A OIDC with approved personal-Google federation | Proposed, blocked pending evidence | Potentially satisfies SET-01 and NFR-004 together; support and permitted configuration unknown |
| Direct Google OIDC, or another provider independently brokering Google | Rejected under current policy | Bypasses mandated organizational identity capability |
| Org A using only organization-managed accounts | Not sufficient as specified | Does not demonstrate personal-Google support required by NFR-004 |
| Local passwords or product-owned identity provider | Rejected under current policy and PRD | Conflicts with SET-01 and personal-Google constraint |

## Evidence required before acceptance

1. Obtain ADR-104 and the applicable Org A platform integration contract from the architecture/identity owner. Record source revision and relevant restrictions; do not invent its contents.
2. Demonstrate a personal Google account completing authentication through Org A in an approved test environment. Verify the application trusts Org A's issuer and audience, with stable subject mapping, rather than accepting a Google token through a bypass.
3. Establish pilot volunteer enrollment, coordinator/admin role assignment authority, account-linking rules, allowed callback configuration, and supported session lifecycle controls. Confirm how any platform-originated revocation is enforced locally.
4. Validate the proposed application session design, including multi-instance invalidation, concurrent sessions and sign-in races. Measure old-session denial within 300 seconds following administrator revocation and verify failure-closed behavior when validity cannot be checked.
5. Record review by the identity/architecture owner and product owner. Approve only a route that preserves both constraints. Global compliance/accessibility gaps B-02/B-03 still gate implementation epics even after this ADR is accepted.

If evidence establishes incompatibility, retain BLOCKED status and document it. The product owner can pursue a PRD amendment; only the organizational authority can change policy (POL-001 requires CTO approval). This task authorizes neither amendment nor an exception, and no approval is requested here.

## Consequences and propagation

Directly blocks FR-001, NFR-004, and final NFR-001 integration. FR-002 depends on authenticated volunteer sessions; FR-003 and NFR-002 depend on verified roles and session enforcement, so they inherit this blocker. Hosted deployment design under NFR-003 can proceed independently of this identity choice, subject to whole-product quality gaps.

Federation adds an external dependency and requires testing the complete sign-in journey for the organization's eventual accessibility criteria. Application session enforcement adds storage and availability dependencies but provides a concrete mechanism for the five-minute bound. No provider SLA, cost, supported federation feature, or compliance status is asserted.
