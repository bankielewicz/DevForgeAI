# ADR-001 — Identity boundary

## Context

[PRD-001](../prd/PRD-001.md) requires volunteer sign-in, personal Google accounts, and administrative revocation of signed-in sessions within five minutes. [POL-001#SET-01](../policy/POL-001.md) mandates Org A Identity Platform (OIDC), cites organization ADR-104, and permits no override. ADR-104 and provider capabilities are unavailable in this repository. The PRD's open identity-provider question is therefore constrained by policy, not a free provider selection.

## Decision direction

Use Org A as the application's authentication provider. Propose personal Google accounts as an upstream identity source through Org A, conditional on documented and tested support. The application trusts the configured Org A issuer and stable subject identifier, rather than directly accepting Google identity tokens or using an email address as an authorization rule.

Keep application authorization and sessions on the server. Store authoritative per-user session generations in shared durable storage, check them on every protected operation, and invalidate older generations atomically on admin revocation. No positive authorization cache is proposed. Recheck live connections and pending protected responses before sending new data. This establishes an application enforcement mechanism; it does not prove provider-session behavior.

Do not let silently refreshed credentials or a still-valid provider session recreate access that should remain revoked. Determine the necessary provider revocation, reauthentication, or other supported integration contract before accepting the decision. The PRD does not say that revoking existing sessions permanently disables an account; enrollment/deactivation is a distinct operational decision.

## Alternatives and consequences

- Direct Google sign-in: meets the account constraint in isolation, but bypasses the mandated identity platform. Rejected under SET-01.
- Local credentials or a different hosted identity provider: conflicts with SET-01 and/or NFR-004. Rejected.
- Org A with confirmed Google federation: preserves both constraints if supported. Proposed, not verified.
- Treating short token expiry alone as revocation: insufficient evidence for all signed-in sessions and renewal paths. Not accepted as proof of NFR-001.

The application can define a provider adapter now, but sign-in and dependent epics cannot be called ready until compatibility, role provisioning, and end-to-end revocation are established. If Google accounts are unsupported, escalate the incompatible requirements to the policy owner and product owner; this ADR cannot grant an exception.

## Acceptance evidence still required

Resolve ARCH-001 GAP-01, GAP-02, and identity portions of GAP-05. Obtain ADR-104's applicable integration constraints. Demonstrate sign-in with a personal Google account through Org A, validate identity claims and callback protections, and measure administrative revocation across multiple sessions, instances, connections, and renewal paths at no more than 300 seconds. All integration checks are **NOT_RUN**. This ADR remains **proposed**; the platform mandate itself is already authoritative.
