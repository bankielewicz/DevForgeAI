# BRN-001 — A neighbourhood tool-lending service for one street

## 1. Context

Project kind: greenfield

The 48 households on one street buy tools they use once, because nobody knows which neighbour already owns a
drill or a ladder. The street organiser says the few loans that happen are arranged in the group chat, and
tools often come back late. Nothing exists yet: no list, no app, no process. The street wants a first release
it can try within a month.

## 3. Target users

| Persona | Job to be done | Current workaround | Pain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neighbour who rarely needs a tool | Do a one-off job at home without buying a tool | Asks in the group chat, or buys the tool | Buys something used once, or waits days for an answer |
| Tool owner | Help neighbours without losing the tool | Lends to close friends only | Tools come back late, damaged or not at all |
| Street organiser | Keep lending going without a full-time job | Reads the group chat and reminds people by hand | Cannot tell who holds what |

## 5. Evaluation method

Diverge-converge: problems were framed first, ideas were generated without judgment, and each idea was then
rated high, medium or low for value, effort and risk. The score is 2 × value − effort − risk, counting high = 3,
medium = 2 and low = 1 (range −4 to 4); a higher score is a stronger candidate for promotion.

The open assumptions are ranked riskiest first: by what breaks among the first-release candidates if the
assumption is wrong (Fatal, Serious or Minor), then by how little is known (low confidence first), then by
assumption number. The first release cut tests the riskiest assumptions with at most three ideas.
